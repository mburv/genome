# Decision log

## Confirmed by the owner in conversation

- D001: Single-player only; no multiplayer development capacity.
- D002: 9×9 DNA sliding puzzle, assemble authentic human genes; long-term goal all genes.
- D003: Every board unique, with meaningful cut-outs/holes and varied shapes rather than repeated templates.
- D004: Adopt v2 direction: contiguous segment rotation with gaps interrupting movement; sequencing, splicing, replication, repair; varied target positions.
- D005: Develop the real game using Codex; the v2 inline prototype was positively received. The discussion about power-ups was also positively received, but the exact costs, rules and rollout remain design proposals.

## Proposed defaults (not explicitly mandated)

- P001: GENOME working title, tagline 'Build what makes you human.' No trademark search completed.
- P002: TypeScript/React/Vite and Capacitor later; validate mobile experience before native framework commitment.
- P003: 12-level sample demo, then full CDS chapters; progressively larger sequence tokens to preserve exact coverage without excessive repetitive puzzles.
- P004: Main production target is a pinned representative human protein-coding catalogue. Clarifies but does not exhaust the original 'all genes' vision. Noncoding/other scopes future, owner review required before final release claim.
- P005: Canonical uniqueness under all rotations/reflections; connected boards; no normal movement across holes; no timer or move limit initially.
- P006: First powers CRISPR, Ligase, Scaffold; level-local energy, proposed costs and effects in GDD. No purchases.
- P007: Offline save/export, no account or analytics, iOS first packaging (web development first), Android later if desired.

## Open design risks / owner review

- Q001: Full-catalogue playtime: even very short gene chapters imply an extremely long campaign. Test token compression and optional discovery routes; never conflate discovery with assembly.
- Q002: Long DNA tiles may feel like generic symbols rather than DNA. Test readable previews, labels, sequence-based equality and whether players enjoy them.
- Q003: Is unique-mask geometry enough? Test target/move diversity, puzzle difficulty and power-ups. Solvable is not necessarily fun.
- Q004: Energy curve, booster balance and combinations; no final costs until playtesting.
- Q005: Dataset inclusion, MANE gaps, release/version policies and redistribution attribution; import and verify actual records. No current full catalogue has been downloaded or validated in this starter pack.
- Q006: Full scope of 'all genes', iOS vs Android launch, final art direction, audio, monetisation (none by default), accessibility and name clearance. Resolve before store release, not before M1.

When a proposed rule changes, record date, change, rationale and impact on existing saves/levels. Do not treat an AI-generated design proposal as owner approval.

## M1 implementation decisions (13 Nov 2026) — accepted by owner after playtest, 13 Nov 2026

- D-M1-01: Stack as P002 (TypeScript strict, React, Vite, Vitest, Playwright, ESLint). Boards are 81-character strings (`A/T/C/G`, `.` = hole) because M1 uses single-base tokens only; M4 multi-base tokens will need a token array. Impact: none on saves yet (schema v1).
- D-M1-02: The three M1 sequencing levels use INS CDS intervals [0,9), [9,18), [18,27). The GDD 12-level table assigns [9,18) to splicing and [18,24) to replication; M2 will re-map. M1 level ids are `m1-ins-01..03`, so M2 levels will not collide; completed M1 ids may need a migration note in M2.
- D-M1-03: Level pack is generated at build time from explicit seeds (`scripts/generate-levels.ts`) and pinned as JSON; a test proves regeneration is byte-identical. Generator version `m1-gen-1`.
- D-M1-04: Mask rules: connected, no immobile cells, every row/column keeps ≥3 playable cells, ≤2–4 dead ends, active-cell ranges per level (tutorial 70–78, others 56–70), canonical uniqueness over all 8 dihedral transforms and min symmetric Hamming distance 8 between any two levels.
- D-M1-05: Certificates are the inverse scramble, then greedily pruned of moves not needed for a win (so the first hint is never a filler-only move). Labelled "estimated", not optimal.
- D-M1-06: Recovery hint = inverse of the last move on a certified path from the generated solved board; any player move that revisits a state on that path truncates it (handles inverses, aliases and full-circle cycles). Hint is verified against the current board before display.
- D-M1-07: Input during the 150 ms slide animation is ignored (not queued). Undo history capped at 100 snapshots. Undo/hint disabled on a solved board; Restart doubles as Replay. Completion credit is a set of level ids (idempotent; replays add nothing).
- D-M1-08: Keyboard: arrow keys slide the selected tile's segment; Shift+arrow moves the selection. Level selection is open (GDD demo rule).
- D-M1-09: Save: localStorage key `genome-save`, schema v1, includes generator version + dataset id. Unreadable/unknown-version saves and invalid boards are backed up to `genome-save-backup` and reported to the player; completions are kept when the level still exists.

## Continuous drag (13 Nov 2026, from owner playtest feedback)

- D-M1-10: Owner feedback: "the whole row should drag with my finger the whole way across". Touch/mouse input is now a continuous drag: after 8 px the gesture locks to the dominant axis; the whole hole-bounded segment follows the finger live, wrapping inside a clipped strip so nothing crosses holes. A one-cell segment does not move on that axis. Rendering is transform-only on rAF; React only updates on whole-cell preview changes and commit.
- D-M1-11: Release snaps to the nearest whole cell (110 ms ease-out; instant with reduced motion). A quick flick (≥0.45 px/ms) that hasn't reached half a cell commits one step; long drags get no velocity bonus so deliberate placements are never overshot. Net rotation is normalised mod segment length to the shortest signed equivalent; net 0 (back to origin or a full turn) is a no-op: no move counted, no undo entry.
- D-M1-12: **One drag = one undoable action and counts as ONE move**, however many cells it travels (keeps long drags from feeling penalised, matches "fast paced"). Arrow buttons, keyboard and hints remain single-step and also count one move each. Engine command `slide {origin, axis, steps}`; the recovery path is extended one single step at a time so hints/certificates remain valid after multi-step drags. Target feedback (strip ticks, outline glow) previews the snapped position live during the drag; completion is only checked on commit. Save schema unchanged (v1).

**M1 accepted (13 Nov 2026).** Owner playtest passed. The continuous drag (D-M1-10..12) is owner-approved: "It feels great. Move onto M2". D-M1-01..12 stand unless replaced below.

## M2 implementation decisions (13 Nov 2026) — accepted by owner after playtest, 13 Nov 2026

- D-M2-01: HBB data is RefSeq NM_000518.5 (MANE Select, GeneID 3043), fetched from NCBI E-utilities, raw record pinned in `data/raw/` with SHA-256. CDS 51..494 = 444 bases including the TAA stop; translation matches /translation (NP_000509.1, 147 aa). The importer is now generic (`scripts/import-genes.mjs`, config in `scripts/genes.config.mjs`); INS is unchanged (byte-identical asset).
- D-M2-02: Campaign = GDD 12-level table: per gene, CDS intervals [0,9) sequencing, [9,18) splicing, [18,24) replication, [24,33) repair, [33,42) sequencing, [42,51) splicing → exactly 51 sample bases per gene. Level ids `ins-01..06`, `hbb-01..06`. Generator `m2-gen-1`.
- D-M2-03: Splicing = 2–3 coding tracks that tile the interval in source order (numbered 1..3, may mix orientations). The split is illustrative, not real exon boundaries (the in-game label was removed in D-UI-10).
- D-M2-04: Replication = template (coding, read 5′→3′) plus its **aligned** complement (A↔T, C↔G, same position, read 3′→5′) placed immediately below (horizontal) or to the right (vertical). Only the template interval counts toward coverage, so complement work never inflates base counts.
- D-M2-05: Repair = two coding tracks on one line with the gap cell between them. The gap is a forced hole in the mask (blocks movement like any hole) and shows "?"; the missing base (from the CDS) is shown on the board and the target panel only after the win. It is described as a reference gap, not a mutation.
- D-M2-06: Difficulty is measured in the drag metric (one move = rotate one segment by any k), matching D-M1-12. Scrambles use whole drags biased towards target segments; certificates are the inverted scramble, pruned drag by drag. Every non-tutorial level must have no solution in ≤ 2 drags (exhaustive BFS proof); hbb-05/06 additionally must have none in ≤ 3 (1.5 M-node check).
- D-M2-07: Bounded solver (`src/generator/solver.ts`): BFS in the drag metric with a node budget (400 k per level at publish, 60 k in the audit). It runs only at build/audit time; results are stored per level (`metrics.solver`), so the UI never runs it. "Optimal" is shown only when proven (found, or lower bound = certificate); otherwise "≥ k (unproven)". When the solver finds a shorter solution than the certificate, that solution becomes the published certificate so hints follow an optimal path. Limit: ~140^d states, so it proves ≤ 3 drags on typical boards and cannot resolve 4–5-drag levels within budget.
- D-M2-08: Ratings (tutorial/gentle/moderate/tricky/hard) are **estimates**: score = 3 × drags (optimum if proven, else the midpoint of bound and certificate) + (tracks − 1) + wrong cells/4 + active cells/40; thresholds 10.5 / 12.5 / 15. Not player data. The pinned ramp goes from 1 → 2 → … → ≥4–5 drags (M1 was 1 / ≤3 / ≤6 single steps, i.e. ≤ 2–3 drags).
- D-M2-09: Masks: new primitives (diagonal, branch) + asymmetric option; `tidyMask` reopens a crossing neighbour for target cells that would otherwise be stuck and drops fragments disconnected from targets. Uniqueness rules unchanged (D-M1-04) and now apply across all 12 levels.
- D-M2-10: Save schema v2 (`datasets` per gene, `discovered`, `legacy.m1Completed`). v1 → v2 migration: the raw v1 save is first copied to `genome-save-backup-m1`; M1 level 1 maps to `ins-01` (same interval, same mode); M1 levels 2–3 have no identical M2 challenge, so they are kept as visible "M1 history" on the map and the player is told they need replaying for credit. Boards in progress from M1 are restarted with a notice. Nothing is dropped silently.
- D-M2-11: A gene is **discovered** when all six of its sample levels are complete. The UI always says "51 of 333/444 coding bases sampled · not yet assembled"; full assembly is M4.
- D-M2-12: Gene card text is the first 1 (INS) or 2 (HBB) sentences of the RefSeq summary in the pinned record, quoted with credit; no disease or medical claims are added.
- D-M2-13: Hints are now whole drags ("drag row 5 right 3 cells"), derived from the single-step recovery path by merging its trailing identical steps; the arrow/keyboard single steps remain. Step animations (arrows, keyboard) reuse the clipped drag strip so wrapping tiles never cross holes.
- D-M2-14: First-time players start directly in level 1; returning players land on the level map. All levels are open (demo rule, D-M1-08).

**M2 accepted (13 Nov 2026).** The owner played the live M2 build and accepted it: "Nice that was good. Move onto M3". D-M2-01..14 stand unless replaced below.

## M3 implementation decisions (13 Nov 2026) — accepted by owner after playtest, 13 Nov 2026

Accepted ("Yeah seems to work great. Let's move onto M4"). The power costs and energy curve (D-M3-01) are still untuned GDD defaults; the owner approved them as-is. Retune only from observed playtest evidence.

- D-M3-01: Costs and economy are the GDD §6 playtest defaults, unchanged: CRISPR ⚡4, Ligase ⚡2, Scaffold ⚡3; start ⚡2 per level; cap ⚡9; scaffold lasts 3 rotations; scaffold gap 1–2 cells. Energy is level-local (no persistent currency). Tune only after the owner's with/without-powers playtest.
- D-M3-02: Powers are available from campaign level 4 (index 3, `POWERS_FROM_INDEX`) and in the Enzyme lab. Levels 1–3 stay pure sliding so players learn the core mechanic first (GDD §8). Every level remains certified solvable without powers (unit test walks every level by certified hints with no powers used).
- D-M3-03: Reward codons are in-frame triples ((CDS offset) mod 3 = 0) lying wholly inside one coding track. +1 the first time a codon becomes correct after a **normal** move. Codons already correct at the start are pre-credited (no reward); codons completed by CRISPR are marked credited with no reward; the complementary strand (replication) never earns. Frame-crossing pieces earn nothing (keeps the reward tied to real codons).
- D-M3-04: Ledger semantics: per attempt, `{earned, credited}` is append-only and survives undo (so undo→redo cannot farm). Spending is a reversible snapshot field: undo of a power refunds it exactly. Restart starts a new attempt (fresh ledger, ⚡2). `available = min(9, 2 + earned − spent)`; paying sets the balance to `capped − cost`, so energy earned above the cap is discarded and can never be spent later.
- D-M3-05: Invalid, cancelled, unaffordable and same-base actions are rejected by the pure engine with a reason, change nothing and cost nothing (no move, no energy, no undo entry). The UI previews every power through the engine (`applyCommand` on a copy) and spends only on Confirm.
- D-M3-06: CRISPR edits any playable, unlocked tile to A/C/G/T. Same base = `same-base` no-op. Each edit raises an `edits` counter that lets save validation accept a board whose base multiset differs from the level by at most `edits` substitutions. The certified recovery path is dropped after an edit (hints switch to search, see D-M3-09).
- D-M3-07: Ligase bonds exactly one complete, correct reward codon (tap any of its tiles). Locked cells behave as holes for every segment on both axes; dragging a locked tile is rejected (`locked`). One bond at a time; it may not overlap an active scaffold. Release is free (no move, no energy) and undoable; undo of the bond refunds it.
- D-M3-08: Scaffold joins the two segments on either side of a 1–2-cell contiguous gap along one axis into one cyclic track that skips the gap (cannot cross a locked cell). Activation counts as a move but does not tick; every successful normal rotation anywhere ticks it; after exactly 3 it expires (`scaffold-expired` event, status message). One scaffold at a time. Undo restores the scaffold and its remaining count. Drags along a scaffold glide across the gap visually.
- D-M3-09: Hints are never invalid. Order: (1) the certified recovery drag when the board is on the certified path and the topology for that segment is unchanged — or "release the bond (free)" when a bond blocks it; (2) a bounded BFS from the current board under current locks/scaffold (depth ≤ 3 drags, 40,000 nodes ≈ 150–180 ms on a desktop CPU; run after a "Thinking…" paint), verified through `applyCommand`; (3) "Undo ×N" back to the newest certified snapshot without a scaffold; (4) free restart. A bad CRISPR edit is therefore always recoverable (e2e test).
- D-M3-10: "Early tutorials grant one free use of each" is implemented as an optional **Enzyme lab** of three generated tutorial levels (lab-crispr: HBB [0,9) sequencing; lab-ligase: INS [9,18) splicing; lab-scaffold: INS [0,9) sequencing with a guaranteed scaffoldable gap). Each lends one free use of its power (refunded on undo, re-granted on restart), adds no coverage and is excluded from discovery and the 12-level count. They reuse campaign intervals of the pinned RefSeq data — no new DNA. The 12 campaign levels are byte-identical to M2 (generator `m2-gen-1` unchanged; campaign id `genome-m3-lab`).
- D-M3-11: Save schema v3: attempts gain `locks`, `scaffold`, `spent`, `freeUses`, `edits` (per snapshot) and `ledger`; settings gain `sound` (default off) and `haptics` (default on). v2 → v3: the raw save is copied to `genome-save-backup-m2`, every completion, discovery and M1 history is kept, boards in progress are upgraded in place (codons already completed by normal moves are credited as earned, capped), and the player sees "All your progress was kept." v1 → v3 still works via v2.
- D-M3-12: Feedback: sound is synthesised locally with Web Audio (no audio files, no network); haptics use `navigator.vibrate` where supported (not on iOS Safari — the settings screen says so). Settings screen (⚙) holds Reduce motion (forced on when the OS asks for it), Sound, Haptics and Reset progress.
- D-M3-13: The generator audit now covers all 15 specs and writes `docs/AUDIT_M3.md` / `docs/audit-m3.json`; `docs/AUDIT_M2.md` is kept as the frozen M2 record.


## M4 implementation decisions (13 Nov 2026, proposed — owner playtest pending)

- D-M4-01 Token model: a board cell holds a *symbol*. Base levels keep A/C/G/T (the M2 campaign and labs are byte-identical, so M3 attempts stay valid). Token levels carry `tokens: {symbol → {seq, kind, label, source}}`, one symbol per distinct sequence (symbols from U+0100). Symbol equality is therefore sequence equality, and the existing engine, solver, win rule, hints, drag and keyboard work unchanged. Labels never decide anything.
- D-M4-02 Readability (Q002): codon tiles show all 3 letters; hexamers show two rows of 3; 12/24-base tiles show the first and last codon (`ATG…GCC`), the length and a per-base colour barcode. If two different sequences would share a label, the label gets a `·2` suffix. Tapping a tile opens an inspector with the full sequence and its source (target, decoy with CDS interval, or CRISPR-edited). The target panel shows a chip per piece plus the full target sequence, with matched pieces highlighted.
- D-M4-03 Decoys: filler tiles are real in-frame slices of the same gene, taken from outside the puzzle interval, never equal to a target piece and tagged with their source interval. No invented DNA.
- D-M4-04 CRISPR on long tiles: the player picks a position inside the piece and a new base (`{cell, base, pos}`). The resulting sequence maps to an existing level symbol when one carries it, so an edit that spells the wanted piece counts by sequence. Otherwise it gets a new custom symbol (U+0500+, stored in the undoable snapshot field `custom`, labelled ✎). Same base is a free no-op and an out-of-range position is rejected. Cost stays ⚡4.
- D-M4-05 Ligase and energy on token levels: each target tile is one reward unit (+1 energy the first time it is placed by a normal move). Ligase bonds a single placed piece. Scaffold is unchanged.
- D-M4-06 Chapters and tiers (Q001): each gene's CDS is split into ordered, non-overlapping, frame-aligned intervals that tile [0, L) exactly, using 10 fixed puzzle shapes (sequence/splice/replicate/repair, 6–12 tiles). Tile size grows through the chapter: tier A is 3,3,3,6,6,6,6,12,12,12 (≤ 579 bp) and tier B is 3,6,6,12,12,12,12,24,24,24 (≤ 1131 bp). The last puzzle takes the remainder, so its final piece may be shorter. Each chapter has 6–10 puzzles (`src/campaign/chapters.ts`, generator `m4-tok-1`, pinned in `chapters.generated.json`, regenerated byte-identically by `validate:levels`).
- D-M4-07 Discovery ≠ assembly. A gene is discovered when its six sample levels are complete (INS/HBB) **or** its chapter opener is solved. It is **assembled** only when the union of completed puzzles covers every CDS base, the reconstruction (built only from the pieces actually completed, complement strands ignored, nothing auto-filled) equals the pinned reference, and its SHA-256 equals the manifest checksum. Replays and complement strands never add coverage, and overlaps count once.
- D-M4-08 Catalogue policy: a candidate list is resolved to its MANE Select RefSeq transcript (NCBI Datasets v2), fetched as GenBank (efetch) and accepted only if all of these hold: KEYWORDS contain "MANE Select"; GeneID and chromosome match; there is an ATG start and a single terminal stop; the length divides by 3; the standard-code translation equals `/translation`; there is no `transl_except`; and the CDS is ≤ 1100 bp (chapter capacity). Result: 12 included and 2 excluded with reasons (GPX1 has a selenocysteine translation exception; LIG1 is verified but 2760 bp exceeds the chapter policy). The count shown in the game is `manifest.included.length`.
- D-M4-09 Save v4 adds `assembled[]`, `tokenGeneratorVersion` and `catalogueId`, and snapshots gain `custom`. v3 → v4 copies the raw save to `genome-save-backup-m3` and keeps every completion, discovery, lab credit, setting and board in progress (given an empty `custom`). New genes join the dataset table. The notice says "All your progress was kept." Assembly claims are re-verified on every load; a claim that fails is cleared with a notice, and the completed puzzles are kept. v1/v2 saves still migrate through the chain.
- D-M4-10 Scale audit (`docs/AUDIT_M4.md`): 107/107 puzzles pass validation, certificate replay and a full certified-hint walk. The solver proved 83 optimal; 24 are unresolved at 60k nodes, with lower bounds and still certificate-solvable. There are 0 duplicate masks, the minimum symmetric mask distance is 8, all 12 reconstructions match their SHA-256, and the slowest solver run takes about 0.65 s. Generation of all chapters takes about 40 s at build time and none at runtime.
- D-M4-11 Estimated chapter duration (model, not playtested): 25 s + 18 s per certified drag + 1.5 s per tile type, ×1.25 for 12/24-base tiers. INS ≈ 19 min (12–31), HBB ≈ 24 min (14–38), RHO ≈ 32 min (19–51). That is longer than "short", so the owner playtest should decide whether to cut shapes or use bigger tiers earlier.
- D-M4-12 Q001 choice: compress with tokens (6–10 puzzles per gene whatever the CDS length, up to 1131 bp) and keep a fast optional discovery route (sample levels or the chapter opener) separate from full assembly. The catalogue stays a curated 12, not "all genes"; longer genes need longer tokens or chapters (LIG1 is the test case). Q002 choice: the readability ladder in D-M4-02, sequence-based equality and the inspector. Whether long tiles still feel like DNA is an open playtest question.
- D-M4-13 Gene-to-ability milestones are deferred. Powers are already available from level 4 without an unlock economy, and there is no designed reward curve. The long-token power semantics (D-M4-04/05) are now ready, so milestones can be added once the owner chooses an economy.
- D-M4-14 Bundle size: the pinned chapter pack is about 680 KB of JSON (the JS bundle is about 1 MB minified and about 0.2 MB gzipped). This is acceptable for the web build; M5 should shard per gene or load lazily.
- D-M4-15 (13 Nov 2026, from owner review: "each 3, 6, 12 or 24 letter combo should have its own unique colour instead of being beige"): multi-base tiles are coloured per sequence by a pure, deterministic module (`src/engine/tokenColours.ts`, no DOM/Date/fetch/Math.random).
  - **Palette.** It uses a fixed OKLCH grid: 7 lightness levels × 5 chroma levels × 60 hues. Each colour gets near-black ink (`#0b1420`) or white text, whichever contrasts more, and only colours with text contrast of at least 4.8:1 (WCAG 4.5 plus a margin) and in the sRGB gamut are kept. That leaves 931 candidates, ordered once by farthest-point sampling in OKLab, so any prefix is well spread.
  - **Assignment.** In each puzzle the distinct sequences (target pieces and decoys) are sorted by sequence and take palette entries 0…n−1. The same sequence therefore has the same colour on the board, in the target chips, under the full-sequence line and in the inspector chip. No two sequences in a puzzle share a colour, and renumbering symbols changes nothing.
  - **Checks across all 107 puzzles.** Up to 65 combos per puzzle; the smallest perceptual distance between any two is ΔE_OK 7.06 (unit test threshold 7; about 2 is just noticeable). All text and secondary text (ellipsis, length badge, `·2`) is at least 4.5:1.
  - **Readability.** Letters on coloured tiles use the single text colour; the per-base colour strip stays, outlined. Each tile also gets a same-hue edge.
  - **Overlays.** Selection and hints keep the white outlines. Matched, bonded and scaffolded tiles get an outer ring separated by a dark gap so the ring reads on any colour.
  - **Unchanged / special cases.** Single-base tiles (A/C/G/T) are unchanged. CRISPR-created sequences that match no reference piece use neutral hatching with the dashed ✎ outline, so they never pose as a palette colour. An edit that spells a real piece takes that piece's colour.
  - **Limits.** Colours are consistent within a puzzle, not across puzzles. With 50–65 combos some neighbouring hues are only moderately different, so the strip, the label and the inspector stay the authoritative identity.

## Visual redesign (13 Nov 2026, from owner direction — "very clean clinical Swiss international style look and colour palette … the game board must always be edge to edge … tiles as big as possible")

- D-UI-01 Clinical Swiss / International Typographic Style.
  - **Palette and surfaces.** A light lab-paper canvas (`#f7f7f5`), pure white panels, stone `#efefea`, hairline rules `#d4d4ce` and carbon `#111111` structural rules.
  - **Type.** Carbon ink with graphite `#52524e` for metadata, in a Helvetica/Arial sans with monospace DNA. Numerals are tabular, micro-labels are uppercase and tracked, and the hierarchy is strictly left-aligned.
  - **Shapes and fills.** Corners are 0–2px; there are no gradients, glows or drop shadows. Grouped controls (d-pad, actions, powers, chapter nodes) are joined with shared 1px rules.
  - **Accents.** Signal red `#d92b2b` (primary action, hints, the GENOME mark), cobalt `#0057b8`, emerald `#007a4d` and amber `#c86a00`, all as flat fields.
  - **Browser chrome.** `theme-color` and the page background are `#f7f7f5`, and the favicon is redrawn flat.
- D-UI-02 Single-base tiles are flat pigment blocks:
  - A = emerald with white text (5.4:1);
  - C = cobalt with white text (6.9:1);
  - G = amber with carbon text (5.0:1);
  - T = signal red with white text (4.9:1).
  - Holes are recessed `#e8e8e2` squares with a fine diagonal hatch. Target rows/columns get a 3px carbon frame with a 2px white inner rule (readable on any tile colour); complementary strands use a dashed cobalt frame, and fully matched targets a green one. A matched tile gets a carbon ✓ square, selection a carbon + white inset double rule, hints a dashed carbon rule plus a red arrow block, Ligase an amber inset, and Scaffold a cobalt inset with dashed cobalt bridges. Power targeting dims non-candidates to 28% opacity.
- D-UI-03 Full-bleed board. Up to 640px (and on wider screens up to the 560px app column) the board is a direct, unpadded child of the app, `width: 100%`, square (`aspect-ratio: 1`), with 1px hairline gutters (the board background shows through as the grid) and 0px outer padding. Every other block keeps a 14px inner margin (10px at ≤ 360px). There is no horizontal overflow: `overflow-x: clip`, and the track frames and badges sit inside the board.
  - Measured tile size before → after: 37.5px → 42.4px at 390×844 (board 366 → 390px wide, left inset 12 → 0); 27.5px → 34.7px at 320×568 (board 272 → 320px); 41.7px → 46.9px at 430×932.
  - Tile type scales with the board: base letters are board/15 (26px at 390); codon/hexamer/long-tile text is board/27, /34 and /37.
  - The board is no longer capped by viewport height, so on short screens the controls scroll below it.
- D-UI-04 Combo colours (D-M4-15) were retuned for the light canvas. The OKLCH grid now uses lightness levels 0.34–0.58 and 0.72–0.90 and chroma levels 0.07–0.23, and every candidate must be at least ΔE_OK 12 from each clinical surface (canvas, white, stone, hole, rule, ink), so a tile never reads as an empty cell or a rule. That leaves 866 candidates. Across all 107 puzzles the smallest distance between two combos is 7.27 (test threshold 7), and all text and badge contrast is at least 4.5:1. Edited (CRISPR) sequences use a neutral white/stone hatch with a dashed carbon rule.

    - Deep inks: red `#b23a35`, blue `#2b5f9e`, green `#2a6e4f` and amber `#8c5a10` (5.5–6.5:1 on paper) are now only used for text, links, dashed gap rules, the lock/scaffold insets and the complement/all-matched track frames, which need solid edges to read on any tile.
- D-UI-06 Combo palette in the pastel register (`src/engine/tokenColours.ts`).
  - **Grid and filters.** Candidates come from an OKLCH grid with L 0.72–0.95 and C 0.035–0.12, sampled at steps of 0.02 L, 0.01 C and 4° hue. Each kept colour must:
    - stay at or below 80% of the sRGB gamut boundary at its hue and lightness, which removes neon such as `#27fdf7`;
    - keep carbon ink `#111111` at WCAG ≥ 7.2:1;
    - sit at least ΔE_OK 6 from the paper, white, stone, hole and rule surfaces.
  - **Selection and ordering.** The first 66 entries (CORE_SIZE, at least the largest puzzle's 65 distinct combos) are chosen by farthest-point sampling, then refined by deterministic max-min swaps, then re-ordered farthest-point so any prefix is well spread. Assignment per puzzle (sorted by sequence) is unchanged, so one sequence has one colour on the board, the strip and the inspector.
  - **Text and edges.** Every combo uses carbon ink with a 1px edge in a deeper tone of the same hue. Secondary text (`…`, `12`) is ink blended towards the fill, and stays at least 4.8:1.
  - **Measured across all 107 puzzles.** There are zero colour collisions. The minimum distance within a puzzle is ΔE_OK 5.01 (rho-c06, 65 combos), against 7.27 with the old vivid grid. Main-label contrast is at least 7.20:1 and badge contrast at least 4.80:1.
  - **Lower test threshold.** The threshold drops honestly from 7 to 5: a soft pastel volume holds fewer well-separated colours, and ΔE 5 is still about 2.5× a just-noticeable difference. Tests also pin the pastel L/C bounds, the deeper same-hue edge and the 7:1 ink contrast.
  - **Caveat.** At the low-lightness end some combos read as muted mid-tones (dusty teal, olive, cornflower) rather than pure pastel. A lighter floor (L ≥ 0.76) would drop separation to ΔE 4.3, so separation won.

## Touch drag on iOS Safari (14 Nov 2026, owner report — "the tile dragging mechanic has stopped working … make the lines draggable again")

- D-INPUT-01 The board no longer re-captures touch pointers.
  - **Root cause.** The touched tile already holds the pointer: WebKit/iOS (and the Pointer Events spec) capture a touch implicitly to the element touched. `onPointerDown` then called `board.setPointerCapture()`. Moving capture from the tile to the board makes WebKit fire `lostpointercapture` at the tile. That event bubbles, and the board's `onLostPointerCapture` treated it as a cancel, clearing the pending drag before the first `pointermove`. So on an iPhone, rows and columns never moved, while taps (select) and the arrow buttons still worked.
  - **Why tests missed it.** Chromium (all existing Playwright e2e) does not fire that event, so every Chromium touch test passed.
  - **Not introduced by a commit in this range.** Built and run side by side, 83ea3d8 (M4), b2f5d5a, a96f3a7 and 34bb756 behave identically in Chromium real touch (works) and under WebKit capture semantics (fails). No CSS, overlay or z-index change in the colour and Swiss commits intercepted the board.
  - **Fix.**
    - Explicit capture is used only for mouse and pen; touch keeps the tile's implicit capture, whose events bubble to the board.
    - `lostpointercapture` cancels only when it targets the board itself.
    - While a board gesture is live, a non-passive `touchmove` listener calls `preventDefault()`. This stops iOS from starting a page scroll or edge swipe now that the page scrolls vertically and the full-bleed board touches the screen edges. `touchstart` is left alone, so taps and power targeting still click.
    - `touch-action: none` is now explicit on tiles, cells and the drag layer (the global button rule is `manipulation`).
    - `overflow-x: clip` moved from `html, body` to `.app`, avoiding root-overflow quirks on iOS.
  - **Regression tests.** `e2e/touch-regression.spec.ts` emulates WebKit's capture hand-over on top of genuine CDP touch input. It covers row and column drags at 390×844 and 320×568 (one move, the board changes, undo restores, no page scroll, every board touchmove default-prevented), a check that a swipe starting off the board still scrolls the page, and a mouse drag. These tests fail on the previous code and pass with the fix.
  - **Engine.** Unchanged: 1 drag = 1 move/undo, flick, 8px axis lock, nudge buttons.

## Solved state and Replay (14 Nov 2026, owner request — completed levels were not obviously locked)

- D-UI-07 Completed levels show a clear Solved state and a prominent Replay.
  - **Cause.** A solved attempt locks the board (no drags, no arrow buttons). The only Replay control sat in a bar under the disabled pad, easy to miss — especially on a phone, and especially when reopening a finished level from the map. That is why a completed level looked like dragging had broken.
  - **Banner.** When a completed level is opened with a solved (or missing) attempt, and after the win card is dismissed, a calm banner sits above the board: a "Solved" kicker, the last solve and stored best in moves, informational stars against par, a coral Replay button, and Next (or Level map on the last puzzle). The disabled pad, undo, hint and restart controls are hidden so the banner is not pushed below the fold. The board stays in its solved arrangement at 78% opacity.
  - **Replay.** Replay is a free restart: the board returns to the starting scramble and is fully draggable again. Completion credit is unchanged (D-M1-07). A new `best` map on the v4 save records the lowest move count (then fewest powers). A worse replay does not overwrite it; a better one does. Schema stays at v4: missing `best` defaults to `{}` and is backfilled from any solved attempt's move count (powers unknown). Invalid / unknown ids are dropped.
  - **Stars.** Informational only. Par is the proven optimum when the solver found one, otherwise the certificate length in drags. 3 stars at or under par, 2 within +2 or 1.5× par (whichever is looser), 1 otherwise.
  - **Locked-drag hint.** Trying to drag a locked solved board pulses the Replay button and sets the status to "This level is already solved. Tap Replay to play it again." (no pulse when reduced motion is on). Mid-replay of a completed level plays normally and still shows "Best N" above the pad.
  - **Engine.** Unchanged and still pure. Restart already started a fresh attempt; the UI now records `best` next to `recordCompletion`.
